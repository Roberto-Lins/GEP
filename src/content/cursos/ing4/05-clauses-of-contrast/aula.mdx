## O que é contraste

A segunda parte da frase contradiz ou surpreende em relação à primeira.
*Está chovendo (expectativa: ficar em casa) + nós saímos (realidade contrastante)* →
***Although** it was raining, we went out.*

## Os conectores + oração completa (sujeito + verbo)

### Although — “embora”
*Although + subject + verb.* No início da frase, usa-se vírgula.
- *Although the ad said it would last for years, mine broke after two months.*
- *I enjoyed the film although it was very long.*

### Even though — forma **mais forte/enfática** que *although*
- *I went to work even though I wasn’t feeling very well.*
- *Even though she is very rich, she doesn’t like spending money.*

### Though — “embora” (mais **informal**), costuma vir no meio
- *I like Ann, though she sometimes annoys me.*

## Os conectores + substantivo / -ing

### Despite — “apesar de”
*Despite + noun / pronoun / -ing.* **Atenção de ouro: NUNCA use “of” depois de despite.**
- *Despite his age, he is still very active.* (substantivo)
- *Despite being 85, she is still very active.* (-ing)

### In spite of — mesmo uso de *despite*, mas **exige “of”**
- *In spite of her age, she is still very active.*
- *In spite of being 85, she is still very active.*

### The fact that — quando a ideia depois for uma **oração completa**
Se quiser usar *despite/in spite of* mas o que segue é sujeito + verbo, acrescente *the fact that*:
*Despite / In spite of + **the fact that** + subject + verb.*
- *In spite of the fact that she’s 85, she is still very active.*
- *Despite the fact that he’s the managing director, he goes to work by bike.*

## Como escolher (passo a passo)

1. Olhe **logo após a lacuna**.
2. Há **sujeito + verbo conjugado**? → *Although / Even though / Though*.
3. Há só um **substantivo** (*the rain*) ou **-ing sem sujeito** (*being*)? → *Despite / In spite of*.
4. Há a expressão **“the fact that”**? → *Despite / In spite of*.

## Como transformar frases

Transformar exige mudar a classe gramatical: verbo ↔ substantivo/-ing.

- *Although it was raining, we went out.* → *Despite **the rain**, we went out.* (verbo → substantivo)
- *She didn’t get the job in spite of **being** a strong candidate.* → *…although **she was** a strong candidate.*
- *Although **he’s** the managing director, he cycles.* → *Despite **being** the managing director, he cycles.*
- *Even though he studied hard, he failed.* → *In spite of **studying** hard, he failed.*
